# Monitoring Your Cluster

Your Orkes Cloud portal gives you **visibility into the health and activity of your cluster** without leaving the dashboard. This page covers:

- **Component uptime history**, how to check whether each core component is currently healthy and its uptime over time
- **DNS reachability and SSL certificate expiry**, how to check whether your cluster's domains resolve and when their certificates expire
- **Actions history**, how to review the actions run on your cluster, who ran them, and when

To send your cluster's metrics to your own observability platform, such as Dynatrace, Datadog, or an OpenTelemetry (OTLP) backend, see [Exporting Advanced Metrics to an External Monitoring Platform](/content/orkes-cloud/monitoring-your-cluster/exporting-metrics).

## Component uptime history

Go to **Clusters**, select a cluster, and open the **Status** tab. For each of your cluster's core components, Conductor, Agent, Workers, and DNS, you can see:

- Its last reported status: **Healthy**, **Not healthy,** or **Unknown** (Unknown appears if the component hasn't reported in yet)
- Its uptime percentage
- A 90-day history of up and down periods; hover over a day to see the downtime recorded that day

## DNS reachability and SSL certificate expiry

Go to **Clusters**, select a cluster, and open the **Status** tab. Below the DNS component's uptime graph, you'll see a status card for each domain configured on your cluster, showing:

- Whether the domain is reachable
- The resolved IP address(es), or "Unreachable at the moment" if none resolve
- The SSL/TLS certificate expiry, such as the date it is valid until, or how many days remain before it expires

## Actions history

Go to **Clusters**, select a cluster, and open the **Actions History** tab. Actions run on your cluster, such as upgrades, rollbacks, restarts, pod log pulls, and multi-region actions, are logged with:

- The operation that was run
- Its execution status
- When it was created and who created it

Select an entry's ID to view full details. Some actions produce a downloadable result, for example a pod log pull, and you can download it directly from that entry's details.
